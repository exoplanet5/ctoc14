# CTOC14 Problem A — Methods survey: multi-target low-thrust asteroid FLYBY tours

Scope: what is known from GTOC4 (the closest published analogue: low-thrust spacecraft, fly by as many NEAs as possible in a fixed window), from the post-2009 re-solutions of GTOC4, from the GTOC5/GTOC11/GTOC12 beam-search and ship-allocation literature, and from the Chinese NEA-flyby work (CAS in-ecliptic PHA tours, CTOC12-B); then a concrete algorithmic recipe for our problem (300 asteroids, 15 yr, Isp 4000 s, Tmax 0.5 N, m0 <= 2000 kg, several spacecraft, J = sum_i (1 + x_i + x_i^2) + N_miss), calibrated against the in-house results in `results/fleet_b300/`.

Provenance and verification legend (this revision, 2026-09-05):

Population difference (computed): GTOC4 catalogue medians a = 1.76 AU, e = 0.48, i = 10.4 deg; 272/1436 with e < 0.3; Opik encounter speed at Earth median 11.5 km/s. Our `MEA.txt`: medians a = 1.90 AU, e = 0.59, i = 13.5 deg; only 23/300 with e < 0.3; Opik speed median 16.5 km/s; 264/300 Apollos, 271/300 with q < 1 AU (`docs/population_analysis.md`). Our catalogue is smaller and hotter (more eccentric and inclined) than GTOC4's, but our spacecraft is ~3x more accelerative, has 46 % more delta-v and 50 % more time, and needs no final rendezvous. The in-house tours confirm the consequence: median junction delta-v 0.79–0.80 km/s per flyby (vs 0.51–0.65 km/s in GTOC4), median leg 105–110 d (vs 72–83 d).

## 1. GTOC4 — problem, results, and what each team did

### 1.1 Problem statement (CNES: Bertrand, Epenoy, Meyssignac, 2009) [V]
Sources: [problem description PDF](http://sophia.estec.esa.int/gtoc_portal/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/gtoc4_problem_description.pdf) (pages 2, 6, 7 read as images); portal page [GTOC 4 – Asteroids billiard](https://sophia.estec.esa.int/gtoc_portal/?page_id=23); parameters also in [arXiv:2508.02904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02904) Table 1.

- Launch from Earth: r = r_E(t_i), v = v_E(t_i) + v_inf, |v_inf| <= 4.0 km/s, 57023 MJD <= t_i <= 61041 MJD.
- Fly by as many NEAs as possible from the 1436-object file `ast_ephem_gtoc4.txt`, then rendezvous with one asteroid of the list not visited before; each asteroid visited at most once; flight time tau = t_f − t_i <= 10 yr; no gravity assists.
- m_i = 1500 kg fixed (not affected by v_inf), m_d = 500 kg, m_p = 1000 kg; Isp = 3000 s; 0 <= T <= 0.135 N, throttleable, any direction; mass constant during coasts.
- Flyby: r_j = r_Aj(t_j) (position only); rendezvous: position and velocity; "the constraints on position and velocity must be satisfied with accuracy of at least 1000 km and 1 m/s respectively (Euclidean norm)".
- J = sum of alpha_j (asteroids visited before the rendezvous); secondary K = m_f. Keplerian Earth and asteroids; two-body Sun; explicit Kepler-equation ephemeris formulas in the appendix.
- The portal: "Scientists would not agree, before this edition of GTOC, on how many asteroids could be encountered during a low-thrust interplanetary mission. The number 44 (in 10 years) appeared to scientific community as a positive surprise."

### 1.3 Methods of the teams (CNES report; [V] = page read as image in this pass, [U] = from the previous reading)
- **MSU (1st, 44) [V, p.3]** — "analysis of the graph of perspective trajectories": Lambert problems from the Earth to *all* asteroids, keeping solutions with **transfer time between 20 and 100 days** that satisfy the v_inf bound; each further leg computed the same way with "selection criterion based on the transfer time and propellant consumption"; **"progressively 2000, 1000 and 500 trajectories are analysed for each trajectory leg"** (a beam search with shrinking width); finally the low-delta-v sequences are converted to low-thrust optimal trajectories. Their impulsive preliminary design had **48 flybys + 1** and the low-thrust conversion kept 44 [V, arXiv:2508.02904]. Papers: Grigoriev & Zapletin, "Choosing promising sequences of asteroids", Automation and Remote Control 74 (2013) 1284–1296, [doi:10.1134/S0005117913080055](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S0005117913080055) (dynamic programming over the sequence, step-by-step optimisation of time and mass [A]); Grigoriev & Zapletin, "Constructing Pontryagin extremals for the optimal control problem of asteroid fly-by", Automation and Remote Control 70 (2009), [doi:10.1134/S0005117909090045](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S0005117909090045).
- **Aerospace Corp. (2nd, 44) [V, p.4]** — "massive grid search" with a simplified dynamics model and impulsive thrusts; legs parameterised by departure time and duration in **15-day increments**; single legs patched into full missions; leg-by-leg local optimisation with the low-thrust engine; then the full mission as one problem in SOCS (sparse direct collocation); **"the grid search results are used again at this step to determine if additional flybys can be added to the mission."**
- **ESA ACT (3rd, 42) [V, p.5]** — three steps: (1) Branch and Prune over chemically-powered flyby trees after selecting 10 rendezvous targets by MOID; "an optimisation of the Lambert's arc joining the possible targets is done and **the algorithm prune out those transfers that exceed the possible acceleration given by a continuous low thrust**" (in GTOC5 the ACT wrote this as a_max = eta Tmax/m with **eta = 0.9**, §2.1 [V]); (2) Sims–Flanagan direct transcription, first in "single phase" mode to rank the thousands of sequences, then maximising final mass; (3) the MPBVP of the indirect formulation solved with IPOPT and smoothing, started from the direct solution.
- **DEIMOS (4th, 39) [V, p.6]** — Lambert between *all pairs* of asteroids in the database, pruned by maximum leg duration and total delta-v; **"this approach leads to trajectories with up to 44 asteroids visited in 10 years"** in the impulsive model; local optimisation of departure/flyby dates and velocities for final mass; LOTNAV (direct) maximising final mass, also varying thrust switches, dates and departure v_inf.
- **GMV (5th, 39) [V, p.7]** — five steps: ballistic Earth-to-many-asteroids initial guess; impulsive trajectories with manoeuvres at the flybys; branch-and-prune on the delta-v budget with date refinement; low-thrust legs by a local optimiser; **a refinement step "to find opportunities to introduce additional asteroid fly-bys"**; final-mass optimisation.
- **JPL (6th, 38) [V, p.8]** — "combinatorial analysis of Lambert's problems": all pairs of asteroids subject to v_inf and flight-time constraints, pruned by total delta-v and by spacecraft orbital elements; initial guesses optimised with MALTO (low thrust as a succession of small impulses, SNOPT); "the most promising solutions are refined by hand". (AAS 09-433 "Spacecraft Trajectory Design for Tours of Multiple Small Bodies" is by Barbee, Davis & Hur-Diaz (Emergent/GSFC), not JPL; it carries the GTOC4 population tables, [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242372244_Spacecraft_Trajectory_Design_for_Tours_of_Multiple_Small_Bodies).)
- **PoliTo + Sapienza (7th, 36) [V, p.9]** — 2006QQ56 chosen for rendezvous (low e, i, a ~ 1 AU); indirect method for **minimum-time Earth -> nodal-intercept legs and nodal-flyby -> rendezvous legs**; these arcs propagated forward/backward to intercept extra asteroids; the automatic procedure "produced trajectories with up to 40 intercepts within the ten-year trip time, but appeared at a large extent inaccurate"; local optimisation of asteroid–asteroid arcs -> 28; replacements/additions using the actual spacecraft position and orbit -> 36.
- **UT Austin (8th, 32) [U]** — impulsive sub-optimal solutions in a global search tree; SQP impulsive optimisation; conversion to finite burns with SQP; verification in Copernicus. (The same group later produced the 45+1 "Johnson Tour", §1.4.)
- **Glasgow/Strathclyde (9th, 29) [V, p.11]** — "first the intersections between the asteroids trajectories and the ecliptic plane for the years 2015 to 2035 are computed. Then Lambert's problems from any asteroid to any asteroid intersecting the ecliptic plane are solved and classified according to the total delta-V", with min/max leg durations; rendezvous body from a list of 18 (0.7 < a < 1.0 AU, e < 0.2, |i| < 5 deg); GA minimising the sum of departure delta-v's; DITAN direct transcription.
- **Thales (10th, 27) [V, p.12]** — branch and bound on **minimum-time** trajectories from the Earth on a two-week departure grid; keep the shortest-transfer subset; recompute minimum-time legs from those asteroids to all remaining ones; repeat until 10 yr; then fuel minimised with flyby/departure dates tuned; all with T_3D (Pontryagin + shooting).
- **Trento (11th, 26) [U]** — greedy beam: per 300-day window keep the 10 best minimum-time targets, expand, repeat.
- **Bremen/PoliMi (12th, 26) [U]** — "reachable ball" in 200 days for fixed steering directions; pick the first reachable asteroid; NUDOCCCS NLP; many restarts over start dates and random initial velocities.
- **MAI (13th, 24) [U]** — asteroids sorted by inclination and ecliptic-crossing time; global optimisation over launch date, v_inf magnitude and in-plane direction using constant-acceleration attainability domains; PMP + homotopy; DIRECT global optimiser.
- **Georgia Tech (14th, 24) [U]** — Lambert combinatorial pruning per rendezvous target (10 lowest-delta-v rendezvous asteroids); "several thousand promising trajectories" as initial guesses for a direct low-thrust code; leg-by-leg differential dynamic programming.
- **TOMLAB (15th, 22) [U]** — pick a rendezvous target; optimal trajectory to it; repeatedly insert an asteroid "in the vicinity of the computed trajectory" and re-optimise (PROPT pseudospectral + SNOPT).
- **VEGA (16th, 20) [U]** — DAG of flyby-sequence prefixes with edges when a flyby can follow another within duration/fuel/max-delta-v limits; PMP for fixed-time fuel-optimal legs.
- **DLR GSOC + FH Aachen (17th, 20) [V, p.19]** — InTrance neural controller; **step 1 "with maximum flyby-distances starting from 1e7 km down to 1e6 km, a time of flight of 10 years, ignoring the final rendezvous"; step 2 repeated with ~1 yr legs and 2e6 km while minimising the sum of flyby distances; then high-accuracy legs one body at a time** — continuation on the miss distance.
- **Astroshape (18th, 20) [U]** — GA over launch date and v_inf; spiral at 50 % thrust along velocity; "teleport" to any asteroid inside a region; direct transcription afterwards.
- **DLR Space Systems (19th, 19) [U]** — domain knowledge only; stay in a single plane.
- **Tsinghua (20th, 18) [V, p.22]** — "a global optimisation process based on a hybrid algorithm of PSO and DE was used to find an optimised trajectory with an optimised flyby sequence"; the result did not meet the accuracy requirements, so a direct local method optimised fuel per sub-phase while satisfying the flyby and rendezvous constraints.
- **Missouri (21st, 15) [V, p.23]** — three sub-searches (launch window/velocity so the spacecraft can "expose itself to the other asteroids"; flyby legs with the least average time of flight; the rendezvous); **"the possibility of turning a Lambert's result into a low thrust result is added as a constraint of picking sequence and time of flight ... at the critical points of the branching process, a modified shooting method is used to double check the feasibility of turning ballistic arc into a low thrust arc"**, i.e. the Lambert solver is replaced by a low-thrust local optimiser where it matters; final indirect optimiser.
- **Beihang (22nd, 13) [V, p.24]** — "the position of the ascending and descending nodes of each asteroid is computed and the corresponding times are deduced. Then the GTOC4 problem is seen as a Lambert's problem between the ascending or descending nodes of the different asteroids"; sequence and delta-v by Tent-map chaotic PSO; BFGS correction of the impulsive -> low-thrust errors.
- **Texas A&M (23rd, 12) [U]** — launch into the plane of the rendezvous asteroid; phasing down-selection; graph of feasible successive flybys; coast–min-time–coast legs.
- **NUAA (unranked, 54) [V, p.26]** — "dynamic programming method combined with a semi-analytical approximate trajectory algorithm": grid coordinate system per leg, a "moving corridor (i.e. domain of controllability)", local optimisation; constraint violations.
- **CHOPIN (unranked, 24) [V, p.26]** — Earth-like rendezvous candidates via multi-revolution Lambert (9, 10 or 11 revs), backward integration from the final asteroid, flybys found by scanning distances to all asteroids at each date, exponential-sinusoid Lambert per sequence, collocation + NLP.
- **CAS (late, 19) [V, p.26]** — **"the flybys occur when the asteroids cross the ecliptic plane"**; first asteroid = smallest Earth distance among the 2015–2025 crossings; same rule for the following ones; low-inclination rendezvous body; **burn–coast between flybys, burn–coast–burn for the last arc**; NLP by SQP.

Common denominator of every team above rank 10: **(i) an impulsive/Lambert graph search over (asteroid, epoch) nodes pruned by delta-v, by TOF and by an acceleration test; (ii) explicit conversion of the best chains to low thrust with a direct (Sims–Flanagan / SOCS / collocation) or indirect (PMP shooting) solver; (iii) post-hoc insertion of extra flybys near the converged trajectory (Aerospace, GMV, TOMLAB, PoliTo).** Five teams (PoliTo, Glasgow, Beihang, CAS, MAI) explicitly used **nodal / ecliptic-crossing intercepts**, the natural device for an inclined, eccentric catalogue (ours: 104/300 with i > 20 deg).

### 1.4 Post-competition improvements of GTOC4
- **45+1 "Johnson Tour" (UT Austin)**, then **University of Jena (Althofer group): 46+1 (2015) -> 47+1 (Sept 2017, D. Wolz) -> 49+1 (April 2018, D. Wolz, "by sort of a miracle")** [V, [althofer.de/space-trajectories.html](https://althofer.de/space-trajectories.html)]. "Essential for the design of our trajectories are three algorithms by Dario Izzo (Lambert solver), Nikolaus Hansen (CMA-ES), and M.J.D. Powell (BOBYQA)"; CMA-ES for the continuous-thrust conversion and global optimisation, BOBYQA for local leg optimisation; details "on request", no refereed paper. Asteroid list: [gtoc4-jena-46.pdf](https://althofer.de/space/gtoc4-jena-46.pdf); optimiser library [fcmaes](https://github.com/dietmarwo/fast-cma-es).
- **Zhang, Guo, Wu, Baoyin, Li, Topputo (2025), "Global Optimality in Multi-Flyby Asteroid Trajectory Optimization: Theory and Application Techniques"** [V, [arXiv:2508.02904](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02904); JGCD [doi:10.2514/1.G009335](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/1.G009335)]. For a *fixed* sequence the multi-flyby OCP with interior-point position constraints is rewritten as an N-stage decision problem whose stage state is **s_k = [t_k; v_k; m_k]** (time, velocity and mass at the k-th flyby carry the whole memory, since a flyby fixes position only) and solved by dynamic programming on a grid, with the bound J_DP <= J* + N eps_max.
  - Impulsive GTOC4 variant on MSU's 48+1 sequence (bi-impulse at flybys, Lambert legs): grid stepped from 32 d down to 1 d with adaptive refinement, 8411.8 s CPU -> **total delta-v 25 210.4 m/s, m_f 636.7 kg, 20.2 kg better than MSU's winner** -> **0.51 km/s per event**. "Even a 1-day fixed grid deviates notably from the optimum": flyby epochs are strongly multimodal.
  - Low-thrust GTOC4 on Jena's 49+1 sequence: grid 0.2 d in time, 50 m/s in velocity, 1 kg in mass, neural estimator for fuel-optimal delta-v and reachability, 35.9 h on a 32-core EPYC + 2 RTX 3090; then an indirect method for the true fuel-optimal legs: **19.9 kg propellant remaining (25 kg better than Jena), average 19.6 kg per leg (~0.58 km/s at ~1000 kg)**; "this suggests the possibility of solutions with additional flybys". Their solution front-loads propellant relative to Jena's, which local optimisers cannot reach — hence global re-timing.
  - Leg table (first 25 legs): launch MJD2000 57938.2 with |v_inf| = 4.00 km/s; **leg durations 21–144 d, mean 75 d; relative flyby speeds 6.9–17.6 km/s (typically 10–14 km/s)** — velocity is genuinely free at flybys. GTOC11 side result: 11.2–646.8 m/s savings per sequence over Tsinghua's winning solutions at 3.3–4.5 s per sequence.
- **Neural surrogates for legs** [A]: Zhang et al., "Neural Approximators for Low-Thrust Trajectory Transfer Cost and Reachability", [arXiv:2508.02911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02911) — inputs = Lambert solution (e, true anomaly, delta-v1, delta-v2 in spherical coordinates), TOF, a_s = Tmax/m0, Isp, non-dimensionalised; 1e8 indirect (MEE, PMP) solutions from a "homotopy ray"; 0.78 % error on delta-v (3.4 m/s), 0.63 % on minimum transfer time (2.6 d); reachability := TOF >= t_min. Follow-up "Pretrained Approximators for Low-Thrust Trajectory Cost and Reachability", [arXiv:2605.26790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26790) [V abstract]: self-similar transformation generalising across a, i and central body; performance improves linearly with log(data) and log(parameters); validated on "a multi-asteroid flyby problem from the GTOC"; models and datasets released open-source. Not reproducible here, but the *feature choice* (delta-v1, delta-v2, TOF, a) is the right one for a hand-made heuristic.
- **DP + beam-search path planning** [A]: Garcia Mateas & Sanchez, "Low-Thrust Automated Asteroid Tour Trajectory Design via Dynamic Programming", AAS 23-217 (2023); "Automated Path-Planning for Asteroid Tours Using Hybrid Dynamic Programming–Beam Search", JGCD 2026, [doi:10.2514/1.G009214](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/1.G009214): Keplerian-TSP on **triplets of Lambert arcs**, optimal-substructure DP on (asteroid, epoch) states with beam pruning; on GTOC4 it "produced thousands of high-ranking solutions for all launch dates considered" (flyby counts not in the abstract; full text paywalled).
- Review with the GTOC/CTOC champions table: Li, Huang, Yang, "Review of optimization methodologies in Global and China Trajectory Optimization Competitions", Progress in Aerospace Sciences 102 (2018) 60–75, [doi:10.1016/j.paerosci.2018.07.004](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0376042118300435).

### 1.5 What GTOC4 tells us quantitatively
- Per-flyby cost with a 0.09–0.27 mm/s^2 spacecraft on a cooler catalogue: **0.51–0.65 km/s and 75–81 d per flyby** (MSU as flown; Zhang re-timed). The winners were both delta-v-limited and time-limited (m_f within 11–55 kg of the floor at 10.0 yr).
- Scaling to us: 47.2 km/s / 0.8 km/s (in-house median junction delta-v on our hotter catalogue) ~ 59 flybys by delta-v; 15 yr / 110 d ~ 50 flybys by time; the search finds 48–49 for the first spacecraft, i.e. **time-limited at ~110 d per leg**. Shortening legs (more thrust authority: 0.5 N vs 0.135 N) is what buys more flybys, not more propellant.
- The impulsive -> low-thrust gap: MSU 48 -> 44 (−8 %), PoliTo 40 -> 28 -> 36, ACT "some chemically feasible chains could not be transformed" (GTOC5); TheAntipodes GTOC12 "difficult to make feasible". In-house: 49 -> 48 (SC1), 44 -> 33 (SC3), 36 -> 26 (SC5). The gap is the single biggest risk in the pipeline and is closed by (i) conservative feasibility rules in the search (eta ~ 0.5–0.6, fuel margin >= 10 %), (ii) re-timing (DP) before conversion, (iii) multi-leg windows in the converter (§3.4).

## 2. Other directly relevant results

### 2.1 Beam search / branch-and-prune heuristics with numbers (GTOC5, GTOC12)
- **ACT, GTOC5** [V, Acta Futura 8 (2014) 45–55, [PDF](https://www.esa.int/gsp/ACT/doc/ACTAFUTURA/AF08/ACT-BOK-AF08.pdf)]: recursive branch-and-prune over chemical (Lambert) representations; candidate list per asteroid = **the first 512 of 7075 asteroids ranked by the simple Edelbaum approximation** (a phase-free ranking); pruning criteria: **(1) a_max = eta Tmax/m_An with eta = 0.9, prune if the Lambert delta-v over the leg time exceeds a_max**; (2) prune if m_An+1 <= 400 kg; (3) prune if T_An+1 >= 16 years; (4)/(5) a tolerated variation/delay corridor with respect to a nominal linear mass-vs-encounters schedule from 4000 kg to 500 kg. Warnings: "strict pruning criteria" make the search greedy, and some chemically feasible sequences "we later could not transform into a feasible low-thrust" trajectory. Their self-flyby leg model (point mass accelerating away then back at a = Tmax/m0: s(t1+t2) = a t1^2/2 + a t1 t2 − a t2^2/2) is the same rectilinear reasoning that gives our "reachable displacement" rule d ~ a t^2/2 for a position-only intercept (§3.2).
- **JPL, GTOC5** [V, Acta Futura 8, 21–27]: rendezvous–flyby–rendezvous building blocks optimised in MALTO; up to the top 200 legs kept per stage; **"local-rank pruning" (top few RFR additions per parent, <= 10 RFRs per trajectory) out-paced "global-rank pruning" beyond ~15 asteroids**; the diversity rule every beam search should have.
- **PoliTo, GTOC5** [V, Acta Futura 8, 29–35]: delta-v from Edelbaum-like formulas at the orbit intersections / minimum-distance points, flight time = delta-m/m-dot plus an empirical flyby time penalty **delta-t_fb that diminishes with spacecraft mass**; separate transfer databases per mass level.

## 3. Recipe for CTOC14-A (with in-house calibration)

### 3.0 Structural facts about our cost function
- One missed asteroid costs exactly 1; one spacecraft costs J_i = 1 + x + x^2, x = m_fuel/1400 in [0, 1]: 1.00 (empty), 1.08 (100 kg), 1.28 (350 kg), 1.75 (700 kg), 2.31 (1050 kg), 3.00 (full).
- Marginal cost of fuel dJ/dm = (1 + 2x)/1400 per kg: **one asteroid is worth 1400 kg of fuel at x = 0 and still 467 kg at x = 1**. Fuel is nearly free; what limits a spacecraft is time (15 yr), acceleration (0.5 N / m) and catalogue geometry. Size the tank to the sequence — but the *search model* must not promise more than 1400 kg (in-house lesson, §3.2).
- A ballistic spacecraft (x ~ 0, J_i ~ 1) that flies by one asteroid is cost-neutral, two is a net gain of ~1; with ~100–350 kg of fuel (J = 1.08–1.28) a 3rd–6th target is often affordable (in-house tail search: m0 = 1000 kg, J = 1.37, 6 leftovers for 342 kg est.; m0 = 850 kg, J = 1.21, 4 leftovers for 208 kg; m0 = 700 kg, J = 1.08, 2 leftovers for 45 kg — `results/fleet_b300/tail_search.log`).
- Time factor k in [0.9, 1]: submit a valid solution early and iterate.

### 3.1 (a) Ephemeris tables
- All 300 asteroids and the Earth are Keplerian: position at any t is analytic. Pre-tabulate r, v on a **0.5-day grid** over the window: 10 958 x 300 x 6 float64 = 158 MB (79 MB at 1 d); keep the analytic evaluator (`ctoc14/kepler.py`: `Ephemeris.ast_state`, `all_ast_states(t)`) for arbitrary t inside optimisers.
- Kepler solver: all catalogue orbits are elliptic with **e_max = 0.947**; use a safe starter (Danby E0 = M + 0.85 e sign(sin M), or Markley's 1995 non-iterative solution + one Newton/Halley polish) and vectorise over (asteroid x epoch); Raposo-Pulido & Pelaez (CMDA 2017, [doi:10.1007/s10569-017-9785-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10569-017-9785-5)) document starters for which Newton is q-convergent at high e. `analysis/population/kepler.py` reproduces the PDF sample lines to 0.5 m (Earth) and 9 m (asteroid 174).
- Pre-tabulate per asteroid (done in `analysis/population/`): ecliptic-crossing epochs with heliocentric distance/velocity — **2731 in-band (0.8–1.6 AU) crossings in 15 yr = 182/yr, 1160 of them from i > 20 deg objects**; 3220 "access windows" (r in band, |z| <= 0.1 AU) = 215/yr, median length 38.5 d (82 d for i <= 10 deg, 24 d for i > 20 deg); 298/300 reachable, 131 and 144 hopeless (N_miss >= 2). Relative speed at the crossings w.r.t. a 1-AU circular orbit: median 16 km/s — irrelevant for a position-only flyby but it sets the timing tolerance (1000 km / 16 km/s ~ 60 s).
- Pre-tabulate the Earth-departure map (`analysis/ballistic/out/earth_to_asteroid_summary.csv`): 296 targets reachable ballistically with v_inf <= 4 km/s, median minimum v_inf 0.98 km/s. That is the MSU/Thales first layer.

### 3.2 (b) Leg model and feasibility heuristics
- **Node model (position-only flybys)**: chain (A_0 = Earth, t_0), (A_1, t_1), ..., (A_N, t_N); ballistic legs are Lambert arcs; the cost concentrates at the flyby nodes as a velocity discontinuity dv_k = |v_in,k − v_out,k| (MSU's bi-impulse-at-flyby model = Zhang 2025 impulsive variant). At Earth the first impulse is free up to 4 km/s.
- **Lambert**: `ctoc14/lambert.py` is a vectorised single-revolution Izzo-2015 solver (12 us/solve). Multi-rev (M = 1) matters only for legs longer than ~1 yr; tour legs are 30–400 d, so M = 0 suffices, but keep the branch for mop-up chains (legs up to 585 d in `tour_sc9.json`).
- **Reachable displacement and deliverable delta-v (computed)** — constant 0.5 N thrust over a leg of length t gives dv = a t and, for a *position* target, a displacement d = a t^2/2:

  Read: at full mass a 30-day leg can absorb a 0.65 km/s junction impulse only if it is applied at the *start* of the leg; a thrust arc that only starts late in a leg moves the arrival point by a t_burn^2/2, not by a t_burn x TOF. This "leverage" is why position-only targeting needs eta < 1.
- **Low-thrust feasibility of a Lambert leg (the eta rule)** used by ACT 2009/2011, Thales, TheAntipodes and `ctoc14/search.py`: a node delta-v is deliverable if dv <= eta a(m) TOF, a = 0.5 N/m. Literature values: **ACT GTOC5 eta = 0.9 (rendezvous legs, thrust spread over the whole leg)** [V]; TheAntipodes 80 d/(km/s) + 10 d ~ eta 0.7 [V]. For position-only targeting: with constant thrust over the leg the reachable displacement is a TOF^2/2, i.e. only about half of a TOF is usable at the end of a leg, and the propellant cost of a junction dv spread uniformly is up to 2 dv — hence the in-house model dv <= eta a TOF with **eta = 0.6** and fuel factor kappa = 1 + dv/(2 a TOF) (`search.py`). At 2000 kg: **0.65 km/s per 30 days** of thrust; at 1200 kg, 1.08 km/s per 30 days.

  **Conclusions: (i) plan with a fuel margin of >= 12 % (search budget ~1250 kg) or eta ~ 0.5; (ii) the 2-leg sliding-window converter is reliable up to junction dv ~ 1.3 km/s and legs <= ~150 d; beyond that thrust arcs span most of the leg, the linearisation is poor, and a 3–4-leg window or a direct transcription with free flyby epochs is needed (§3.4); (iii) legs with dv > 2 km/s should be split by the search via an intermediate (possibly already-visited) node or rejected; (iv) long coasting legs (> 300 d, mop-up chains) over-consume by ~2x in the converter because the Lambert junction is far from time-optimal — re-time them (DP) before converting.**
- **Phasing delta-v rule of thumb (derived here)**: to shift the arrival epoch at a fixed point of a ~1-AU orbit by delta-t using n full revolutions, change the period by delta-t/n; with P ∝ a^{3/2} and dv ~ (v/2)(da/a) for a tangential burn, dv_phase ~ v delta-t/(3 n P) = **0.027 km/s per day of shift per revolution** (v = 29.8 km/s, P = 365 d). A 10-day phase error costs 0.27 km/s if corrected over one revolution, 0.09 km/s over three — cheap compared with a 0.8 km/s junction; this is why early tours (SC1–SC3) with many in-band crossings to choose from are cheap and late tours on the depleted catalogue are not.
- **"Phase-free" targeting (two tiers)**. The phase-free question is purely geometric: how close does the asteroid's orbit come to the spacecraft's current osculating orbit (their MOID), and where. The phased question is whether both bodies reach that point at the same time. GTOC4 teams used both faces: ACT chose rendezvous candidates by MOID [V]; PoliTo, Glasgow, Beihang and CAS used the asteroid's ecliptic node — which *is* the MOID point for an in-ecliptic spacecraft — as the intercept point [V]; Perozzi et al. 2001 call this "orbit first, phase second". Recipe: (1) for each asteroid tabulate its ecliptic-crossing epochs and, for a spacecraft on a non-ecliptic orbit, the pair-MOID point and the asteroid's epochs of passage through it (one per period); (2) for the spacecraft, the epoch it passes the same point (one per spacecraft period); (3) the leg is cheap when |delta-t| is small or fixable by the phasing rule, otherwise use the Lambert branch. Edelbaum-style orbit-to-orbit delta-v (ACT/PoliTo GTOC5) is the rendezvous analogue and over-estimates flyby cost badly (it charges the full velocity match); for flybys the phase-free proxy is MOID + phasing, not Edelbaum.
- **Better leg surrogates, in increasing cost**: (1) the linear orbital indicator |dr/dT + dv| as a pre-filter (§2.1); (2) Lambert dv with the eta/kappa rule (current); (3) "Lambert with one free intermediate impulse" for nodes failing (2); (4) a small regression on a few thousand converted legs with features (dv1, dv2, TOF, a) as in arXiv:2508.02911 — the conversion logs already contain the training data (Lambert dv vs achieved propellant per leg).
- **Mass bookkeeping in the search**: m_{k+1} = m_k exp(−kappa dv_k / 39.227 km/s); a(m) updates automatically; include the departure excess over 4 km/s.
- **Per-flyby budget**: GTOC4-class costs (0.5–0.65 km/s per flyby) would allow ~70 flybys per full tank in delta-v terms; our catalogue costs 0.8–0.9 km/s per flyby (in-house median) -> ~50 in delta-v terms; the time budget at ~110 d per leg allows ~50 legs in 15 yr. **Both limits land at ~48–50 for the first spacecraft, which is exactly what the beam search finds (49, 48)**; later spacecraft on the depleted catalogue fall to 44, 39, 36, 29, 25, 19 because the well-phased targets are consumed first (§3.5).

### 3.3 (c) Sequence search (beam search with DP-style dominance)
- **State**: (last asteroid, epoch t_k, arrival velocity v_k, mass m_k, visited bitset, cumulative dv, count). **Expansion**: for each unvisited asteroid j, candidate arrival epochs from (i) a TOF grid t_k + [15, 400] d (MSU used 20–100 d; Aerospace a 15-d grid; TheAntipodes 5-d steps) and (ii) the pre-tabulated nodal / 1-AU crossing epochs of j inside that window (the CAS/Glasgow/Beihang trick: captures most cheap intercepts with far fewer Lambert solves). Vectorised Lambert over epochs; dv_k = |v_k − v_dep|; eta-feasibility; mass update. `search.py` keeps the best `n_per_target = 2` TOFs per target per parent (JPL's local-rank diversity rule).
- **Scoring**: score = n_flybys − w_fuel (fuel/1400) − (t/15 yr) (in-house w_fuel = 0.7–1.5); the CAS 2022 "combined dv-and-time" rule is the same; GTOC5 experience: a deterministic beam with a good cost beats stochastic variants. Add a **look-ahead** term (ACT GTOC12): cheapest feasible next leg from the candidate node, or the number of in-band crossings reachable within the next 200 d.
- **Dominance pruning (DP flavour)**: bucket states by (asteroid, epoch rounded to ~5 d); keep only the Pareto-best in (count, mass, remaining time) per bucket (Garcia Mateas/Sanchez hybrid; NUAA "corridor"). Removes the duplication that makes plain beam search wasteful.
- **Beam width**: in-house 300 (x3 pre-sort) already gives 49; MSU kept 2000 -> 1000 -> 500; CAS 2022 saw convergence between 200 and 10 000 [U]. Cost per expansion ~ 300 targets x ~40 TOF = 12 000 Lambert solves ~ 0.15 s; **one full-tank tour takes 30–90 s** (`fleet_b300.log`: 87 s for SC1, 22 s for SC8). Depth 40–60. Widening to 2000–5000 is affordable (minutes) and is the cheapest way to buy 1–3 more flybys per spacecraft.
- **Restarts** over launch epoch (in-house 0–400 d step 20 d; extend to the first 3–4 yr for later spacecraft, as Thales' two-week grid) and over v_inf direction / spacecraft orbit family (stay at 1 AU ecliptic; 0.9–1.3 AU eccentric; inclined 5–10 deg). Keep the top ~10 000 full chains in a pool for the multi-spacecraft ILP (§3.5).
- **Re-timing** each promising chain with the fixed-sequence DP of Zhang et al. (state (t, v, m), grid 4–8 d with adaptive refinement, bi-impulse Lambert legs, O(N_t^3 N)): recovered 7 % of dv on MSU's GTOC4 sequence (27.0 -> 25.2 km/s) [V]; on our 40 km/s tours that is ~3 km/s ~ 3–4 flybys' worth of fuel, or exactly the 10 % margin that SC1/SC4 lacked.

### 3.4 (d) Converting a chain to a continuous-thrust trajectory (|r_sc − r_ast| <= 1000 km)
- **Per-leg problem**: given (r_k, v_k, m_k, t_k), reach r_j(t_{k+1}) with free velocity and free t_{k+1}, min fuel. Write the flyby as a **2-D B-plane miss constraint**: with v_rel = v_sc − v_ast at the nominal epoch, the along-track component of the miss vector is removed by shifting the declared flyby time (delta-t = −(delta-r · v_rel)/|v_rel|^2), so only the two components perpendicular to v_rel are real constraints (what `lowthrust.py` does). Each leg of n thrust samples therefore has 3n − 2 spare degrees of freedom for fuel minimisation. Three tiers:
  1. **Burn–coast per leg (CAS 2009 [V])**: full thrust right after the flyby for tau = dv/a along a fixed inertial direction, then coast; unknowns (tau, 2 angles, burn delay) vs 2 miss constraints; `scipy.optimize.least_squares` from the Lambert guess. Fast, feasible, worth submitting early for the k-factor.
  2. **Multiple shooting on the validator's thrust nodes** (in-house `convert_tour`; equivalent to Sims–Flanagan with 1-day segments). Variables: samples T_j at t_j = t_k + j h (h >= 8640 s; 1 d in-house), interpolated by the validator's sliding-window cubic Lagrange rule; state propagated by a vectorised fixed-step RK4 (validator-exact integrator for the final check). Constraints per flyby: 2 B-plane components; bounds |T_j| <= 0.45 N at samples plus the interpolant overshoot check (`arc_max_thrust`). Objective: sum |T_j| h/(Isp g0) (fuel; IRLS turns it into weighted least-norm steps). Sensitivities: delta-r(t_f) = Phi_rv(t_f, t_j) T_j h/m_j, with Phi_rv the two-body state-transition sub-block along the reference (closed form, or integrate the variational equations alongside the state) — replaces the finite differences of the in-house code; rectilinear approximation Phi_rv ~ (t_f − t_j) I is what the reachable-displacement table encodes. Sizes: a 110-d leg = 330 variables; **a 2-leg window = ~660 variables and 4 constraints** (least-norm, milliseconds); a whole 48-leg tour = ~16 000 variables — too big for SLSQP's dense BFGS (2 GB), fine for `trust-constr` with sparse Jacobians or for the sliding window. In-house: 2-leg window, miss tolerance 300 km, <= 12 iterations, converged legs frozen, **~10 min per 48-flyby tour**. Two upgrades suggested by the literature: **free the flyby epochs inside the window (the DP shows they are the multimodal variables)** and **widen the window to 3–4 legs when a junction exceeds ~1.3 km/s** so thrust can be spread before the previous flyby (the GTOC4 SF tool optimises legs "in pairs ... maximising the feasibility of the subsequent legs" [A]; TheAntipodes SCP: 5–10-d segments, log-mass, ~0.3–0.5 s per ship [V]).
  3. **Indirect (PMP) with position-only interior constraints** (MSU, PoliTo, Thales, VEGA in GTOC4 [V]; Zhang et al. 2025 finish with it [V]). H = lambda_r · v + lambda_v · (−mu r/r^3 + T u/m) − lambda_m T/(Isp g0); u = −lambda_v/|lambda_v|; bang-bang on the switching function; energy -> fuel homotopy (Jiang–Baoyin–Li 2012). Interior-point conditions for N = r(t_k) − r_A(t_k) = 0 (Bryson & Ho §3.5): lambda_r(t_k^−) = lambda_r(t_k^+) + pi_k (3 multipliers), lambda_v and lambda_m continuous, and for a free flyby epoch H(t_k^−) = H(t_k^+) + pi_k · v_A(t_k). Costate transformation across nodes (Chen–Li–Baoyin 2018) optimises many legs at once. Best final quality; only after tier 2 gives a good guess.
- **Continuation on the miss distance** (DLR GSOC's InTrance: 1e7 km -> 1e6 km -> target [V]) is a robust way to bring a badly-converging leg in (the SC5/SC6 failures started at ~1.8e8 km misses).
- **Insertion pass** (Aerospace, GMV, TOMLAB, PoliTo [V]): after conversion, scan the converged trajectory against all unvisited asteroids (distance < ~0.05 AU at any time) and try to insert them with a local re-optimisation of the two neighbouring legs — this is how the GTOC4 winners went from ~40 to 44.
- **Validation and output**: integrate with the validator's exact thrust model (`ctoc14/validator.py`; `solve_ivp(method="DOP853", rtol=1e-12, atol=1e-9)` as the independent check); design miss <= 300 km to leave margin (16 km/s relative speed -> 1000 km = 60 s of timing); check |T| <= 0.5 N *between* samples (cubic overshoot; `docs/thrust_interpolation_rule.md`); sample spacing >= 8640 s; arcs end instantly; write `%.17g`.
- Mass: m-dot = −|T|/(Isp g0); Isp g0 = 39.227 km/s; 0.5 N -> 1.2746e-5 kg/s = 1.101 kg/day.

### 3.5 (e) Partitioning 300 targets among N spacecraft
- **Greedy-sequential** (in-house `run_fleet_search.py`; the CTOC12/GTOC standard): best chain on the full set, remove its asteroids, repeat while gain = n − J_i > threshold. In-house outcome (`results/fleet_b300.log`): 49, 48, 44, 39, 36, 29, 25, 19 flybys for 8 full tanks (launch days 0–220, ends 13.6–15.0 yr), then a 3-flyby 192-kg spacecraft; SC10 (2 flybys, 224 kg) has gain −1 and stops the loop; **292 covered in the search model, J = 35.00**. The degradation (−3 to −7 per spacecraft) is the cost of greed: later chains pay 1.4–2.8x the dv per flyby (median junction dv 0.79 -> 2.19 km/s) and become unconvertible.
- **Set-packing ILP over a pool** (ACT GTOC12: max-weight independent set, SCIP [V]; TheAntipodes/Astrodynamics 2025: BIP with HiGHS [V]; CTOC13-B champion for vehicle sequences [U]): keep many chains c (asteroid set S_c, cost J_c from *converted* propellant), solve min sum_c J_c y_c + sum_a (1 − sum_{c ∋ a} y_c) s.t. sum_{c ∋ a} y_c <= 1 for every asteroid a, y_c in {0,1}; `scipy.optimize.milp` (HiGHS) handles 1e4–1e5 columns. Then re-optimise the chosen chains against each other (insertion of asteroids freed by the others).
- **Balanced construction** instead of pure greed: run the beam search for the k-th spacecraft with a *penalty* (not exclusion) on targets the previous chains reach cheaply; or build all N chains simultaneously in one beam whose state is the tuple of N spacecraft (the multi-ship variant used in GTOC12).
- **Tank sizing**: for the last full-tank spacecraft and the mop-ups, x is a decision variable; J_i(x) is convex, so scan m0 in {650, 700, 850, 1000, 1200, 2000} (in-house tail searches) and keep the best (flybys − J_i).
- Expected structure: 6–8 full or near-full tanks each taking 25–50 asteroids in different orbital "phases" (launch dates spread over the first 2–4 yr, different v_inf directions), plus cheap launches (J ~ 1.05–1.4) for 2–6 leftovers each. Cost estimate for 290+ covered: 8 x 3 + ~4 x 1.2 + ~6 misses ~ 35–39 (in-house search-model estimate 35 with 8 misses; validated total not yet assembled because SC1/SC3–SC5/SC7 are over 1400 kg after conversion). 131 and 144 are certain misses.
- Heavier spacecraft are slower (0.25 vs 0.42 mm/s^2 at 2000 vs 1200 kg) but fuel is cheap, so prefer full tanks for the long tours; the acceleration penalty is absorbed by longer early legs.

## 4. Practical numerical advice (Python, numpy/scipy only; no pykep, numba, pygmo)

1. **Units**: km, s, kg internally (matches the submission); mu = 1.32712440018e11 km^3/s^2, AU = 149597870.7 km, day = 86400 s, year = 365.25 d. Non-dimensionalise (AU, TU = yr/2 pi, mass/2000 kg) inside optimisers and convert at I/O.
2. **Kepler**: vectorised Newton/Halley on E with Danby's starter (or Markley 1995 + one polish); wrap M to [0, 2 pi); solve all 300 asteroids x all grid epochs in one array call (`analysis/population/kepler.py`: max residual 8.9e-16 rad over 300 x 10 958 with e up to 0.947). Spacecraft coasts: Lagrange f, g in the universal anomaly chi (`ctoc14/kepler.py::propagate_twobody`, vectorised over dt, ~1e-6 km over 15 yr), so near-parabolic states after an aggressive launch never break the propagator; the classic references are Battin (1999) §4.4 and Vallado, "Fundamentals of Astrodynamics and Applications", alg. 8.
3. **Lambert**: Izzo 2015 — Householder iterations on the Lancaster–Blanchard-like variable x with a piecewise initial guess, **2 iterations for the single-revolution case, accuracy of Gooding at lower cost**; multi-rev by locating the T(x) minimum first ([arXiv:1403.2705](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.2705), CMDA 121 (2015) 1–15, [doi:10.1007/s10569-014-9587-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10569-014-9587-y)). Transcribe from the pykep or [lamberthub](https://github.com/jorgepiloto/lamberthub) sources; vectorise over the TOF array for fixed (r1, r2) and over targets for fixed epochs — the beam-search hot loop is "one departure state x 300 targets x 40 TOFs = 12 000 problems", which runs as three or four masked numpy Householder sweeps; expect 1e5–1e6 solves/s per core (in-house 12 us/solve). Handle the theta -> 0, pi degeneracies (Battin's treatment). Validate against `solve_ivp` propagations and classic textbook cases. Solver review: de la Torre Sangra & Fantino, [arXiv:2104.05283](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.05283); Russell, "On the Solution to Every Lambert Problem", CMDA 131 (2019) 50.
4. **Candidate shortlisting**: compute the linear orbital indicator |dr/dT + dv| for all 300 targets x the TOF grid in one broadcast (30 flops each), keep the best ~60 (target, TOF) pairs per state, run Lambert only on those. ACT used 1000-of-60 000 shortlists in GTOC12 with this indicator [V].
5. **Beam search in Python**: numpy structured arrays for states; whole-level broadcasting (targets x epochs); `multiprocessing.Pool` across beam chunks or across launch-date restarts (embarrassingly parallel); create the ephemeris table *before* the Pool so fork shares it as a module global instead of pickling 100+ MB per task; bucketed dominance pruning in a dict keyed by (asteroid, epoch // 5 d).
6. **Optimisation**: `least_squares` (TRF, x_scale) for burn–coast feasibility; IRLS least-norm for the 2–4-leg thrust-sample windows; `minimize(method="SLSQP")` up to ~1000 variables, `trust-constr` with sparse Jacobians beyond; `scipy.optimize.milp` for the set-packing ILP; `differential_evolution` or the fixed-sequence DP in place of CMA-ES (not in scipy; the Jena results used it for chain-level global optimisation of epochs).
7. **Validation**: `solve_ivp(fun, (t0, t1), y0, method="DOP853", rtol=1e-12, atol=1e-9, dense_output=True)` with the thrust reconstructed by the validator's sliding-window cubic Lagrange rule; compare line by line against the in-house `validator.py` and `tools/validator2.py`; require < 1 km, < 1 m/s, < 0.01 kg at every line; enforce sample spacing >= 8640 s and |T| <= 0.5 N at 100 sub-samples per interval. Agreement of two integrators at the 1e-3 km level on the same rows is the evidence that the file is self-consistent.
8. **Timing of flybys**: declare the numerically converged closest-approach epoch (1-D Newton on d(t)^2 using the dense output), never the planned one; 60 s of timing error at 16 km/s is the whole 1000 km budget.
9. **Reference numerics**: Sims & Flanagan 1999 ([NTRS 20000057422](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20000057422/downloads/20000057422.pdf)); Markley, "Kepler equation solver", CMDA 63 (1995) 101–111; Danby, Fundamentals of Celestial Mechanics (1992) §6.6; Bryson & Ho, Applied Optimal Control (1975) §3.5 (interior-point constraints); Casalino, Colasurdo, Sentinella, "Indirect Optimization Method for Low-Thrust Interplanetary Trajectories", IEPC 2007.
